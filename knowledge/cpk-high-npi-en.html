Methodology & Standards

Why can a high Cpk process still lose control in mass production?

Use within and overall variation to evaluate NPI release evidence and understand why a high Cpk alone cannot prove production stability.

About 3 min readProcess Capability Analysis ToolUpdated 2026-07-31

Quick takeaways

  • A high Cpk does not prove that a process is ready for stable mass production.
  • Cp/Cpk describe within-subgroup variation; Pp/Ppk describe overall variation.
  • Capability indices must be read with control charts, MSA, and data representativeness.
  • A Cpk–Ppk gap is an investigation clue for between-group or time-related variation.
  • Capability indices support release decisions but do not replace them.

Core conclusion

A strong Cpk may represent only a short, single-condition run. NPI release also needs evidence about overall variation, stability, and representative production conditions.

Engineering note

A Cpk–Ppk gap points to a need to investigate between-group variation; it does not prove one specific root cause.

Common misconception

Cpk above 1.33 does not automatically mean the process can be released.

Within and overall variation in NPI release

During NPI trial-production reviews, suppliers often submit an attractive Cpk such as 1.33 or 1.67. A high Cpk, however, does not by itself prove that the process is ready for stable mass production.

Equipment can look good within one shift, one material lot, one operator, and a short production window. Across multiple shifts, lots, and continuous production, material differences, equipment adjustments, environment, and operator effects can emerge.

Capability analysis should therefore consider Cp, Cpk, Pp, Ppk, control charts, and the way the data was subgrouped.

Cp/Cpk versus Pp/Ppk

The key difference is not simply “short term” versus “long term”; it is how the standard deviation is estimated.

Cp/Cpk: within-subgroup variation

Cp and Cpk generally estimate process variation from rational subgroups. They are closer to asking:

Under relatively consistent production conditions, can this process make good product?

Cp reflects the relationship between process spread and specification width. Cpk also considers whether the process mean is shifted toward one specification limit. A high Cp can still produce a lower Cpk when the mean is off centre.

Pp/Ppk: overall variation

Pp and Ppk use the overall standard deviation of all observations. Overall variation may include shift, material-lot, equipment, cavity, tooling, changeover, operator, environment, and time-related mean shifts.

Under the actual production conditions represented by the collected data, how does this process perform?

When trial data covers representative conditions, Pp and Ppk are often closer to expected mass-production performance.

Why can Cpk be high while Ppk is low?

If each short time window is stable but day/night shifts, material lots, or cavities have different means, combining the subgroups widens the overall distribution.

  • Within-subgroup spread is narrow;
  • overall spread is wider;
  • Cp/Cpk are high;
  • Pp/Ppk are materially lower;
  • control charts may show shifts, trends, or stratification.

This may mean the equipment can perform under one condition, while the broader process cannot yet hold the same performance across production conditions. The source may be material control, changeover management, parameter settings, shift differences, or execution—not necessarily equipment precision.

Read control charts with capability indices

Capability indices cannot determine whether a process is stable. A Cpk of 1.67 does not prove future performance if the control chart shows a sustained rise, cyclic variation, or points beyond control limits.

Before capability analysis, confirm statistical control, special causes, subgroup mean shifts, real production order, and coverage of the main future-production conditions. A control chart answers “is the process stable?” while a capability index answers “how does current variation relate to specifications?” They are not substitutes.

Four common result patterns

1. Cp/Cpk and Pp/Ppk are both low

Within and overall variation may both be large, or the mean may be far from target. Check the measurement system, equipment, tooling, process window, centering, and fit between product tolerance and process capability. Do not release directly, and do not widen tolerances merely to improve Cpk.

2. Cp/Cpk are high, but Pp/Ppk are much lower

The process can perform under consistent conditions, but between-group variation is large. Investigate shifts, material lots, changeovers, equipment, cavities, and tooling. Consider first-piece confirmation, locked parameters, changeover validation, lot monitoring, stratified analysis, and reaction plans.

3. Cp/Pp are high, but Cpk/Ppk are low

Spread may be small, but the mean is close to one specification limit. Re-centre the process rather than focusing only on reducing variation.

4. Cpk and Ppk are both high and close

This usually suggests small within and between-group variation, a reasonable mean position, and no obvious long-term drift in the sample. If the control chart is stable, MSA is reliable, and trial data represents future conditions, this is useful NPI release evidence—but not the only release criterion.

Two prerequisites before interpreting capability

Is the measurement system reliable?

If repeatability and reproducibility are inadequate, the indices may contain measurement error rather than true process variation. Confirm appropriate MSA for critical dimensions and characteristics.

Are the subgroups rational?

Rational subgroups keep products produced under similar conditions together while allowing differences across time, shifts, lots, or equipment to emerge. Mixing cavities, equipment, or lots inside one subgroup can remove the engineering meaning of within variation. Conversely, a one-hour sample from one lot and one condition may not represent mass production.

For NPI release, do not ask only whether Cpk reaches 1.33

  1. Did the data come from a stable process?
  2. Can the measurement system distinguish product variation?
  3. Does the sample cover shifts, equipment, cavities, and material lots?
  4. Is there a material Cpk–Ppk gap?
  5. Does the gap indicate group shifts, drift, or measurement issues?
  6. Do the trial conditions represent mass production?
  7. Are controls and reaction plans sufficient for critical characteristics?

Capability indices can support release decisions, but cannot automatically replace them. Consider product risk, failure consequence, improvement plans, temporary controls, and follow-up verification.

Summary

Cp/Cpk are closer to asking:

Under relatively consistent conditions, can this process make good product?

Pp/Ppk are closer to asking:

Under the actual production conditions represented by the data, how does the process perform?

For NPI, the important question is not whether the Cpk looks attractive, but whether the data is stable and representative and whether process controls can be sustained in mass production.
