Quality Engineering Intelligence Insights

How industrial AI is changing data analysis and decision-making in quality engineering

Perspectives on manufacturing quality intelligence from AVEVA's AI strategy.

About 8 min readAI & Quality EngineeringUpdated 2026-08-03
Quality EngineeringIndustrial AIManufacturing AIQuality IntelligenceSmart ManufacturingAI AgentDigital Transformation

Quick takeaways

  • The value of industrial AI is expanding from information access to engineering decision support, built on connecting data, knowledge and workflows.
  • Data connection is a prerequisite for industrial AI: quality problems require complete engineering relationships among products, processes, suppliers and historical experience.
  • Quality tools are moving from outputting analysis results to supporting complete problem-analysis workflows, and AI agents will drive cross-functional collaborative analysis.
  • Engineering responsibility and professional judgment remain with the quality team; AI improves analysis efficiency but does not replace critical quality decisions.

Core conclusion

The core of industrial AI is not replacing engineers, but creating tighter connections among data, engineering knowledge and historical experience to support faster, more transparent and verifiable engineering decisions.

Engineering note

Quality decisions involve product risk, customer impact, regulatory requirements and business factors. Critical judgments still require engineers to evaluate them in the specific business context; AI provides support, but engineering responsibility belongs to the quality team.

Common misconception

Accumulating data is not the same as decision efficiency. If the engineering relationships behind the data still have to be built manually, AI will struggle to truly participate in complex quality problem analysis.

Industrial AI is entering engineering decision scenarios

Manufacturers have been through decades of digitalisation. From product development and production to supply chain management and quality control, companies keep building new systems to record and manage the data generated across business processes.

However, when companies face complex engineering problems, accumulated data alone has not fully solved the problem of decision efficiency.

For quality teams, a customer complaint, a process abnormality or a supplier quality risk investigation usually requires engineers to search across multiple systems. They check historical abnormalities in the quality system, confirm process changes in the production system, understand material and manufacturing conditions at the supplier side, and then combine personal experience to judge the scope of the problem and the direction for improvement.

Companies have large amounts of data, but the engineering relationships behind that data still need to be built manually.

Recently, AVEVA presented its industrial AI direction at AVEVA World Milan 2026. AVEVA's core argument is that industrial AI should serve real industrial scenarios by connecting data, knowledge and workflows to help people make faster, more transparent and verifiable decisions across design, manufacturing, operations and optimisation. AVEVA also emphasised that industrial AI applications should revolve around actual business metrics and require transparency, traceability, and the ability to connect evidence, assumptions and historical records.

This trend is highly relevant to quality engineering, because quality work has always centred on risk identification, problem analysis and engineering judgment.

The value of industrial AI is expanding from information access to engineering decision support

In its presentation, AVEVA showed an evolution path for industrial AI capabilities. From early information assistance, to generative AI, to proactive AI agents and goal-driven AI, industrial software is exploring how AI can participate more deeply in complex business processes.

This change reflects a shift in what manufacturers need from AI.

In the past, companies wanted AI to help employees find information faster, such as looking up equipment documents, searching technical files or summarising operating guides.

As AI capabilities improve, companies are beginning to look at how AI can participate in engineering activities, such as assisting design optimisation, analysing operating conditions, spotting potential risks and supporting people in planning next actions.

Quality engineering follows a similar direction.

Companies already use a wide range of quality analysis tools.

  • Process capability analysis tools calculate metrics such as CPK and Ppk to judge how well a process meets specification requirements;
  • SPC tools identify abnormal signals in control charts to help engineers detect process changes;
  • MSA tools evaluate how the measurement system affects quality judgment;
  • Reliability tools analyse life data to support product reliability assessment.

These tools address the problems of data analysis and calculation.

In real quality improvement work, however, engineers still need to understand the engineering meaning behind analysis results. For example, a process capability shortfall requires judging whether the problem comes from increased process variation or a shift in process centre; confirming whether the impact is concentrated in a specific equipment, material batch or supplier; and deciding whether to adjust the control plan or carry out further validation.

The direction of industrial AI is pushing toward tighter connections between analysis results and engineering actions.

Data connection determines whether industrial AI can truly serve engineering scenarios

AVEVA made a very important point: before industrial intelligence can be realised, companies must first solve the problem of data preparation and data connection.

Manufacturers usually have already accumulated large amounts of industrial data, including equipment operating data, time-series data, engineering models, production events and historical records. However, this data often lives in different systems and is managed under different business logic.

AVEVA's direction includes an Industrial Knowledge Graph and Industrial Ontology, which build relationships among data so AI can understand the connections between industrial objects, attributes and events.

This idea has direct implications for quality engineering.

Quality problems usually involve information from multiple dimensions.

A product failure may relate to failure modes not fully identified during design, but it may also relate to supplier material changes, manufacturing process drift, test coverage or historical improvement actions.

If this information is stored separately in different systems, engineers can only rely on personal experience to connect it.

The future of quality intelligence requires building more complete engineering relationships among product structure, failure modes, supplier performance, process parameters, test results and historical improvement experience, so that AI can help engineers understand the impact scope and potential risks of a problem.

From single quality tools to complete problem-analysis workflows

Today, most quality software is designed around individual engineering tasks. CPK tools focus on process capability; SPC tools focus on process stability; MSA tools focus on the measurement system; 8D tools focus on the problem-solving process.

These tools play an important role in quality engineering, but real problem-solving usually requires engineers to connect multiple analysis steps.

For example, when an abnormal trend appears in production, engineers need to judge whether the abnormality represents a real risk, confirm the impact scope, look for possible failure mechanisms and design a verification plan.

AVEVA's Industrial AI Assistant demonstrates a new application direction. The Assistant can answer questions based on knowledge materials, support natural-language queries over industrial data, call related tools, and increase result transparency by citing sources.

Similar capabilities applied to quality engineering can help engineers use existing quality assets more efficiently.

  • In process capability analysis, AI can help explain possible factors behind CPK changes and point out data that needs further confirmation;
  • In handling customer complaints, AI can connect similar historical problems to help engineers quickly understand past actions and validation results;
  • In 8D analysis, AI can help check whether the problem description, root cause analysis, corrective actions and validation evidence form a complete logic.

The future direction of quality tools will gradually move from simply outputting analysis results to supporting complete engineering workflows.

Agentic AI will drive collaborative analysis of complex quality problems

AVEVA further introduced its Agentic AI direction in its future plans, including collaboration between different AI agents and connecting external AI capabilities through open interfaces.

This direction maps well to complex problem analysis in quality engineering.

A major quality problem usually involves multiple disciplines, including data analysis, failure analysis, reliability assessment, supplier investigation and risk management.

Future quality AI workflows may be completed collaboratively by multiple specialised agents.

  • A data analysis agent can help identify abnormal trends;
  • A knowledge agent can search historical failure cases;
  • A reliability agent can help assess life-cycle risk;
  • A problem-solving agent can help organise the investigation process.

Engineers still need to participate in critical judgments, because quality decisions involve product risk, customer impact, regulatory requirements and business factors that must be evaluated in a specific business environment.

AI can improve analysis efficiency, but engineering responsibility and professional judgment remain with the quality team.

Quality engineers need to strengthen the ability to move from data analysis to engineering judgment

The development of industrial AI will change the focus of quality engineers' daily work.

In the past, engineers spent a lot of time finding data, preparing reports and searching historical materials.

As AI capabilities are integrated into quality processes, engineers can devote more effort to problem definition, risk assessment, validation design and driving improvement.

The core capability of quality engineering is still built on understanding products, manufacturing processes and failure mechanisms. AI can help engineers access information faster and analyse problems more systematically, but final quality judgments still require engineering experience and actual business context.

Competitive quality engineering teams of the future will need to further strengthen their ability to turn data into engineering insight, and engineering insight into improvement actions.

Blendex Labs Insight

The industrial software direction shown by AVEVA provides a reference worth noting for the digitalisation of quality engineering.

In the past, quality digitalisation focused more on recording data, managing processes and performing analysis. With the development of industrial AI, companies are beginning to explore how to create tighter connections among data, engineering knowledge and historical experience.

For quality engineering, this means future software tools will need to take on more roles in assisted analysis and knowledge connection. Methods such as CPK, SPC, MSA, reliability analysis and 8D have long been validated; the new challenge is how to combine these methods more effectively with actual company data and engineering experience.

When quality data can be linked with products, processes and historical knowledge, engineering teams will be able to understand problems faster, assess risk more systematically and develop more reliable improvement plans.

The direction of industrial AI will ultimately move quality engineering from an analysis model that relies on personal experience toward a new model that combines data support, knowledge reuse and engineering decision assistance.
