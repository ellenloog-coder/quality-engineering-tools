How AI is changing the way quality engineering works

Data, experience and engineering judgment will determine how deeply AI is applied in quality analysis.

About 8 min readAI & Quality EngineeringUpdated 2026-08-01

Quick takeaways

  • The value of AI is helping engineers use data, experience and analysis methods more efficiently, not replacing engineering judgment.
  • The main barriers to enterprise AI adoption are data foundations, system connections and knowledge capture, not the tools themselves.
  • Future quality work will shift from collecting data and writing reports to identifying risks, testing assumptions and supporting engineering decisions faster.

Core conclusion

The focus of enterprise AI adoption is shifting from simply using tools to building the foundation that allows AI to participate in real work.

Engineering note

AI analysis results are for engineering reference only; final decisions still require professionals to combine product risk, manufacturing process and actual context.

Core viewpoint

The value of AI in quality is not replacing quality engineers' engineering judgment; it is helping engineers use existing data, experience and analysis methods more efficiently.

Today, the challenge companies face when exploring AI is not a shortage of tools. Large amounts of quality data are scattered across different systems, engineering experience depends on individual accumulation, and historical problems and improvement experience are hard to reuse effectively.

Quality engineering is inherently a field that depends heavily on data and experience. From process capability analysis and abnormality investigation to supplier quality management and reliability analysis, engineers need to combine data results, product characteristics, manufacturing processes and historical experience in their judgment.

AI will not change the core logic of quality engineering, but it will change how engineers access information, analyze problems and support decisions. In the future, high-value quality work will go beyond collecting data and preparing reports; it will use data and experience to identify risks faster, validate assumptions and make more accurate engineering judgments.

Reading the original article: why do companies need to rethink the foundation for AI?

A recent Reuters report on Capgemini noted that companies will continue to invest in IT modernisation over the coming years. The reason is that more and more companies are finding that what limits AI from going deeper into business is not only model capability, but whether their existing data foundation, system architecture and business processes can support AI in real work.

One important trend this article reflects is: the focus of enterprise AI adoption is shifting from simply using tools to building the foundation conditions that allow AI applications to work.

Decades of digitalisation have led companies to build large numbers of business systems, including ERP, PLM, MES, QMS and various specialised applications. These systems help companies record business information, manage processes and improve operating efficiency.

But these systems were originally designed around manual operations. Employees obtain information through the systems and then combine it with their own experience to make judgments.

When companies want AI to take part in more complex business tasks, existing problems gradually emerge: scattered data, weak connections between systems, and knowledge that depends on individual experience.

Companies need not only to store data, but also to strengthen the ability to connect data, so that information from different sources can be analysed with business context and support real decisions.

Capgemini therefore believes companies will need to keep modernising IT, improving data management, optimising system connections and adjusting business processes to create a more stable foundation for AI applications.

The Reuters report on Capgemini discusses changes in enterprise IT development rather than the application of one specific AI tool. But the trend is also relevant to quality engineering, because quality work by nature depends on data, processes and engineering experience.

What does this trend mean for quality engineering?

Quality engineering has always been a field highly dependent on data and experience.

From validation analysis in product development to abnormality investigation in manufacturing, supplier quality management and reliability analysis, engineers need to combine data results, product characteristics, manufacturing processes and historical experience.

In many companies, however, this quality information is still scattered across different systems and files.

For example, manufacturing process data may come from MES, statistical analysis results from SPC tools, and test results from laboratory systems, while quality problem records may exist in 8D reports. How similar past problems were handled depends more on individual engineers' experience.

This is not only a problem of system fragmentation; it is also a problem of different data forms.

Process data in systems such as MES and SPC is usually structured and can be used directly for statistical analysis, while 8D reports, failure analysis records, reliability test reports, engineering specifications and historical improvement experience mostly exist as documents.

In the past, this unstructured engineering knowledge was hard for systems to use effectively. Much experience relied on individual engineers searching, reading and remembering, and companies struggled to continuously reuse accumulated problem-analysis methods and improvement experience.

As AI technology develops, companies are exploring how to connect scattered data and engineering materials more effectively, so that historical problems, engineering cases and analysis methods can contribute to handling new problems.

Therefore, when applying AI in quality, the real problem to solve is not simply letting AI automatically generate an analysis report, but how to combine existing data, engineering experience and analysis methods more effectively.

How is AI changing the way quality engineering works?

Many judgments in quality engineering are not simple data calculations.

For example, whether a process abnormality needs escalation, whether a supplier issue needs broader verification, and whether a test result is sufficient to support product release all require engineers to judge based on product risk, manufacturing process and the actual situation.

This judgment ability comes from long-term engineering practice, not from a single data result.

The value of AI in quality engineering is more likely to appear in helping engineers quickly access relevant information, connect historical experience, spot data trends and support engineering judgment.

To be clear, AI will not replace statistical methods and engineering analysis tools in quality.

Methods such as SPC, Cpk and reliability analysis still rely on mature statistical theory and engineering models for their core calculation logic. The value of AI is not to recalculate these metrics, but to help engineers better understand data changes, connect historical cases, look for potential causes and support follow-up verification and improvement after the analysis is done.

In other words, traditional quality methods handle rigorous calculation and evaluation, while AI helps engineers use analysis results more efficiently in their judgment.

In the past, quality engineers spent a lot of time collecting data, organising materials and searching historical cases before starting their analysis.

As AI technology develops, these repetitive information-handling tasks may gradually decrease, allowing engineers to devote more effort to problem definition, risk judgment, verification design and evaluating improvement actions.

For example, during quality abnormality analysis, AI can help engineers quickly connect similar past problems, organise relevant data changes and provide analysis references; in process capability analysis, AI can help identify abnormal trends so engineers can spot potential risks; and in supplier quality management, AI can combine historical quality performance and improvement records to provide more evidence for risk judgment.

But the final engineering judgment still needs to be made by professionals.

From problem solving to risk prevention: AI's broader impact on quality engineering

Today, many quality activities still focus on analysis and improvement after a problem occurs, such as identifying the abnormality, analysing the cause, defining corrective actions and verifying the improvement.

Further application of AI may push quality engineering from reactive analysis toward front-end prevention.

Past problems, market feedback, customer complaints, supplier quality performance and manufacturing abnormality data can all become important inputs for product development and validation.

For example, historical failure modes can help engineering teams re-evaluate risk during design; supplier quality trends can influence supplier selection and verification strategy; and market problem data can feed back into product validation plans.

This means the value of AI is not only helping companies solve problems that have already occurred, but also helping engineering teams reduce problems that may occur in the future.

Data and engineering knowledge will become an important foundation in quality

Quality data by itself does not equal quality capability.

Companies usually accumulate large amounts of process data, test data, problem records and improvement cases, but if this information lacks connections and organisation, it is hard to support engineering decisions continuously.

Likewise, engineering experience is an important asset in the quality field.

Many excellent engineers can quickly identify the direction of a problem not because they master a single fixed method, but because they have built up long-term understanding of products, materials, processes and failure modes.

For example: why does a certain type of abnormality need further verification? Why can a certain process capability result not be accepted directly? Why does a certain supplier change require re-evaluating risk?

These judgments usually come from extensive engineering practice.

In the future, companies need to pay more attention to capturing this experience so that historical problems, analysis methods and engineering judgment logic can be reused continuously.

The role of AI is not to replace this experience, but to help companies organise, connect and apply existing knowledge more effectively.

How can companies start exploring AI in quality engineering?

Companies do not need to build complex platforms from the start. A more practical approach is to begin with existing quality scenarios.

For example, they can organise failure modes and improvement experience from historical 8D analyses, identify process trends from SPC data, analyse failure patterns from reliability test results, or look for risk changes in supplier quality records.

These scenarios already contain a lot of data and engineering experience; the key is making this information easier to access, analyse and reuse.

Therefore, the focus of exploring AI in quality engineering is not adding a new tool, but gradually improving how existing data is used and turning scattered engineering experience into more efficient problem-analysis capability.

Only when companies can connect data, capture engineering knowledge and define analysis methods can AI truly become a supporting capability for quality engineering work.

Summary

The Reuters report on Capgemini reflects a change that is already happening: enterprise attention on AI is shifting from simple tool usage to how AI can participate more deeply in actual business.

For quality engineering, this means the future value of AI is not only improving work efficiency, but helping engineers use quality data, engineering experience and analysis methods more effectively.

A truly valuable quality AI application does not replace engineers in making judgments; it helps engineers reduce repetitive information handling and devote more effort to problem definition, risk analysis, verification design and improvement decisions.

Future improvements in quality engineering capability will depend more and more on whether companies can effectively use existing data, experience and engineering methods, so that knowledge accumulated in the past truly supports new problem analysis and engineering decisions.
