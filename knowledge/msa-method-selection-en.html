How should I choose Gage R&R, Kappa or Cg/Cgk?

Start with the data type—continuous measurement or categorical judgment—then decide whether you need a short-term gauge check or a complete measurement-system study.

About 3 min readMeasurement System AnalysisUpdated 2026-06-21

Quick selection

  • Continuous measurements: Gage R&R.
  • Pass/fail or other categorical judgments: Attribute Agreement / Kappa.
  • Short-term repeatability and bias of one gauge: Cg/Cgk.

Core conclusion

Use Gage R&R for continuous data, Kappa for categorical agreement, and Cg/Cgk for a short-term check of one gauge. They answer different questions and are not interchangeable.

Engineering note

Interpret measurement-system results with product risk, characteristic importance, tolerance, intended use, customer requirements and the consequences of misclassification.

Common misconception

A passing Cg/Cgk result only shows that one gauge performs well under controlled conditions. It does not prove that the complete measurement system is acceptable.

Gage R&R: the complete continuous measurement system

Gage R&R applies to length, thickness, weight, torque, gap, voltage and strength measurements. It separates equipment repeatability, appraiser reproducibility and part-to-part variation.

Study design

A typical study includes multiple parts, multiple operators and repeated measurements of each part. Parts should cover the realistic process range. A Crossed Gage R&R is common when every operator can measure the same parts; a Nested design may be needed for destructive tests or when operators cannot measure the same parts.

Kappa: agreement for categorical judgments

For pass/fail, scratch/no scratch, defect grades or color judgments, use Attribute Agreement Analysis; Kappa is a common agreement statistic. Check repeat agreement, between-appraiser agreement, agreement with a reference standard, false accepts, false rejects, missed critical defects and category representation.

Cohen’s Kappa is commonly used for two appraisers; Fleiss’ Kappa supports multiple appraisers. Do not interpret one Kappa value alone because category proportions can affect it.

Cg/Cgk: a short-term gauge check

A Type 1 Gauge Study normally uses one operator, one gauge and one reference part under controlled conditions. Cg focuses on repeatability; Cgk considers repeatability and bias from the reference value.

A high Cg with a low Cgk can indicate stable repeatability with systematic bias. Cg/Cgk does not cover operators, parts, locations or real production conditions, so it cannot replace Gage R&R.

A simple selection flow

  1. Categorical result: use Attribute Agreement / Kappa.
  2. Continuous result and one gauge only: use Cg/Cgk.
  3. Continuous result with operators, parts and method: use Gage R&R.
  4. Repeatable samples usually support a crossed design; destructive or non-repeatable samples may require a nested design.

Common selection errors

  • Using Cg/Cgk instead of a complete Gage R&R;
  • Running Gage R&R on pass/fail data;
  • Checking only overall agreement and not error direction;
  • Using parts that do not represent the real process range;
  • Letting appraisers know reference answers in advance;
  • Treating a statistical threshold as the only conclusion.
